## 1. Feature Context

- [ ] `p2` - `cpt-frontx-feature-mfe-host-communication`

### 1.1 Overview

The host runtime routes actions to microfrontend targets through an actions-chains mediator keyed by target identifier and action type, and that routing reaches any target regardless of how many nesting levels separate sender and target: each registry automatically propagates its admitted targets to its ancestors and escalates an unresolved dispatch to its own parent, so the mediator chain composes transitively up to the shell. A narrow parent–child capability bridge gives child microfrontends exactly the participation capabilities they need, delegating each to the registry and its mediator, while the property channel carries no solution-specific vocabulary. Handing a chain to either surface is acceptance-only: the call accepts the chain or refuses it synchronously, reports nothing about what the chain then did, and keeps observation of each action's settlement inside the runtime executing that action. Each action executes where its target lives: where a node's target sits in another runtime, the current runtime hands the node over together with its continuations and is then done with it, and the runtime that executes an action dispatches the continuation its outcome selects from itself. That is what lets one declared chain execute the same way wherever it was dispatched.

### 1.2 Purpose

This feature details the host–MFE dispatch mechanism and the child-facing bridge surface that together realize `cpt-frontx-fr-mfe-host-communication`, including the registration-propagation and escalation mechanism that makes dispatch reach a target at any nesting depth without widening the bridge surface. Action admission is delegated to the injected type-system provider rather than embedded format knowledge, and runs at the registry authoritative for the addressed target, applying `cpt-frontx-principle-agnostic-core`. The dispatch semantics detailed here — acceptance or synchronous refusal, settlement observed only by the runtime executing each action, a node handed across a hop together with its continuations under a delivery that either refuses at the call or accepts, and a bound resolved per node from that node's own declaration or the authoritative target domain's default — are what `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-origin-invariant-chain-execution` (MFES-8) rests on.

### Dispatch Actions Chain to MFE Target

- [ ] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-frontx-flow-mfe-host-communication-dispatch-chain`

**Actor**: `cpt-frontx-actor-project-developer`

**Success Scenarios**:
- Developer dispatches an action chain; the call accepts it and yields nothing to await; the executor routes it to the registered handler, follows the success continuation, and records the completed execution path in its own diagnostics
- Developer dispatches an action chain whose node targets a microfrontend in another runtime; the runtime hands that node over together with its continuations and is then done with it; the receiving registry where the target lives executes the node under its own bound and dispatches the continuation its outcome selects from itself

**Error Scenarios**:
- The chain envelope is structurally malformed — its node graph closes a cycle, or a node declares a per-action timeout the platform cannot schedule as written — or the developer's own dispatch capability is unusable at the moment of the call; the dispatch is refused synchronously at the call and no execution is accepted
- No handler is registered for the target and action type after exhausting the keyed, hierarchy-derived, catch-all, downward forwarding-entry, and (when the registry is not the shell) upward escalation tiers; the chain selects its declared fallback branch, and where it declares none the executor records the unhandled node in its own diagnostics
- The far side of a hop a resolved cross-hop route crosses refuses the delivery at the call — the bridge is inactive or disposed, no receiver is wired, the receiving copy does not recognize the internal transport protocol version, or the receiving registry is disposed; the node fails at the delivering runtime as the hop's unavailability, with its cause recorded, the delivering runtime dispatches the node's declared fallback from itself, and the refusal leaves no side effect at the far side
- A node the far side of a hop accepted then fails there — a missing handler, an admission failure, a handler that throws, synchronously included, or a timeout; the far side records the failure and dispatches the node's declared fallback from itself, and nothing reaches the delivering runtime
- Target entry does not declare the dispatched action type; the node fails before handler resolution and the chain selects its declared fallback branch
- The target extension is registered but not currently mounted; the delivery through its inactive bridge is refused at the call, so the node fails explicitly as the hop's unavailability with a target-inactive cause, distinct from a missing handler, and the delivering runtime dispatches the node's declared fallback branch

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - Developer assembles an actions chain out of nodes that each carry an action identifying the target and action type and may declare that action's own timeout, linked by the `next` and `fallback` continuations the chain declares - `inst-assemble-chain`

4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** any of those is invalid - `inst-refusal-branch`

5. [x] - `p1` - Runtime creates the chain's executor state, reserves what the first executable node needs, and returns nothing to the developer; every step below is the executor's own - `inst-accept-and-reserve`
6. [ ] - `p1` - Executor delegates action admission to the type-system provider of the registry authoritative for the action's target, which validates the action against its registered schema - `inst-admit-action`
7. [x] - `p1` - Runtime checks that the target entry declares the action type in its receivable-action set; infrastructure lifecycle actions are exempt - `inst-decl-check`

    3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the far side accepts the delivery - `inst-flow-delivery-accepted`
       1. [x] - `p1` - The receiving registry carries the chain on from admission of that node, and every failure from then on is answered there; the delivering runtime holds, bounds, and receives nothing for the node - `inst-flow-accepted-continues`
12. [ ] - `p1` - **IF** the resolved handler is local, runtime reserves the node against its own target for the duration of its attempt - `inst-register-inflight`

16. [ ] - `p1` - Runtime releases the node's reservation against its target once that node's attempt settles - `inst-untrack-inflight`
17. [ ] - `p1` - The runtime executing the chain's last reached node concludes the chain in its own settlement record — completion flag and execution path — which serves its branch selection and its diagnostics and reaches no emitter - `inst-return-final`

## 3. Processes / Business Logic (CDSL)

### Mediator Keyed Dispatch and Recursive Chain Execution

- [ ] `p2` - **ID**: `cpt-frontx-algo-mfe-host-communication-mediator-dispatch`

**Input**: An actions chain whose nodes may each declare that action's own timeout, an execution path accumulator, and the chain's diagnostic context — handed to the acceptance operation by an emitter, or handed over across a hop, as a node together with its continuations, by the runtime that delivered it

**Output**: Internal to the runtime executing each node only — a settlement record carrying a completion flag, the accumulated path, and an optional failure class, which that runtime uses to select the node's `next` or `fallback` branch and to attribute the chain in diagnostics; or, for a node whose target lives in another runtime, a hand-over of that node with its continuations that the far side either refuses at the call or accepts. The acceptance operation that admitted the chain yields nothing whatever to its emitter, no runtime that handed a node across a hop receives anything back for it, and no part of this record is reachable from either

**Steps**:
1. [x] - `p1` - Validate the chain envelope before accepting any execution: every node carries an action object with a string target, a `next` or `fallback` present on a node is itself a well-formed chain node, and the node graph holds no cycle. This structural validation is the runtime's own responsibility, because the injected type-system provider admits individual actions and never the chain envelope handed to a dispatch - `inst-validate-envelope`
2. [x] - `p1` - Validate a per-action timeout a node declares: it must be a positive, finite, integer count of milliseconds inside the range a platform timer schedules as written, and a value outside that form is refused rather than clamped, because that bound is the only bound the node's execution carries and one the platform cannot schedule as declared would not enforce what the node declared - `inst-validate-action-timeout`
3. [x] - `p1` - Validate that the emitter's own dispatch capability is usable at the moment of the call: a disposed bridge, a bridge that is already inactive, and a dispatch callback that was never wired are each unusable - `inst-validate-emitter-capability`
4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the envelope, a per-action timeout a node declares, or the emitter's dispatch capability is invalid - `inst-refusal-check`
   1. [x] - `p1` - Refuse the dispatch synchronously at the call, carrying the refusal class, and accept no execution; a refusal is never handed back as a value or as a rejected promise an emitter could await, because it reports that the call was not a dispatch at all rather than reporting on a chain - `inst-refuse-sync`
5. [x] - `p1` - Create the executor's own state for this chain: the execution-path accumulator that records each node the chain reaches, and the diagnostic context every refusal and node failure of this chain is attributed by - `inst-create-executor-state`
6. [ ] - `p1` - Where the first executable node executes locally, reserve what it needs — its reservation against its own target — before the accepting call returns, so a chain dispatched alongside a runtime transition, an entity's own destruction stage above all, cannot lose its target between the call returning and the executor beginning to act; reserve nothing for a first node bound for another runtime, because this runtime hands that node over rather than executes it - `inst-reserve-first-node`
7. [x] - `p1` - Return nothing to the emitter and carry every step below on the executor's own, reporting nothing about what the chain then does - `inst-accept-yields-nothing`
8. [x] - `p1` - **IF** this registry receives a node handed over across a hop by the runtime that delivered it, together with its continuations and the chain's diagnostic context - `inst-receive-hand-over`
   1. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the handed-over envelope carries an internal transport protocol version this copy does not recognize, or this registry is disposed - `inst-receive-refusal-check`
      1. [x] - `p1` - Refuse the delivery at the call, before taking anything, so the refusal has no side effect here and the delivering runtime meets it as the hop's unavailability; a copy that met an unrecognized protocol version records a diagnostic naming the version it met and the version it implements - `inst-receive-refuse`
   2. [x] - `p1` - Otherwise accept the delivery synchronously: validate what was handed over, and take what the node needs — its reservation against its target, where the node executes here — before the delivery call returns. Accepting runs no handler code on the delivering runtime's call stack: the node executes only after that call has returned - `inst-receive-accept-reserve`
   3. [x] - `p1` - Acceptance transfers the sub-chain — the node, its continuations, and the correlation identity, origin, and path so far it carries — to this registry, which continues it from admission below as any executing runtime does. Every later failure of that node — a missing handler, an admission failure, a handler that throws, synchronously included, or a timeout — is this registry's, recorded here and answered by the `fallback` this registry dispatches from itself; nothing after acceptance surfaces back through the delivery call, so no node has its `fallback` run on both sides of a hop - `inst-receive-transfer`
9. [x] - `p1` - Delegate action admission to the type-system provider of the registry authoritative for the action's target — not the registry the dispatch came from, whose provider state is its own — and perform it inside the failing node's failure boundary so the chain's declared `fallback` covers an admission failure as it covers any other node failure; a target identifier the emitter's own provider knows nothing about is therefore a chain failure and never a refusal, since forwarding advertisements deliberately carry opaque identifiers - `inst-delegate-admit`
10. [x] - `p1` - Resolve the handler when the node executes, never earlier. A node that holds a reservation against its target — taken at acceptance, or before its attempt — keeps that target's handler reachable until the node's attempt settles, even where the target is retired in the meantime: retirement excludes the target from new dispatches only, and never turns a standing reservation into a missing-handler failure. The retired target's handler registrations are removed once the reservations standing against it have drained, and the transition that retired it does not wait for them - `inst-reservation-keeps-handler`
11. [x] - `p1` - Look up the handler for the `(targetId, actionTypeId)` pair in the keyed handler registry - `inst-keyed-lookup`
12. [ ] - `p1` - **IF** no keyed handler is found for the pair - `inst-no-keyed`
    1. [x] - `p1` - Match the dispatched action type against each registered key through the type system's derivation check, in either direction, and use the first matching handler - `inst-hierarchy-lookup`
    2. [x] - `p1` - Look up the per-target catch-all handler; the catch-all tier enables forwarding to child domains whose action vocabulary is not enumerated in the parent - `inst-catchall-lookup`
    3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** no hierarchy-derived or catch-all handler matches, look up a downward forwarding entry for the target identifier, recorded through registration propagation from a descendant registry — the same distinct cross-hop route shape as the escalation tier below, not a plain `ActionHandler`, since it carries the node together with its continuations and the chain's diagnostic context into the hop, where the registry authoritative for the target admits, bounds, and executes the node; exclude any forwarding entry whose bridge equals the tagged arrival edge of the action being routed, if it carries one - `inst-forwarding-entry-lookup`
    4. [x] - `p1` - **IF** no forwarding entry resolves and the registry holds an inbound bridge (that is, the registry is not the shell), resolve the escalation tier: the cross-hop route the parent registry minted once for that extension when it first linked its bridge (`inst-mint-escalation-on-link`), reached through the bridge itself — distinct in shape from a plain `ActionHandler`, since it carries the node together with its continuations and the chain's diagnostic context into the hop, where the parent registry admits, bounds, and executes the node or routes it onward, and dispatches its continuation from itself. This runtime hands the node over through that route (`inst-hand-over-node`) and waits on, bounds, and receives nothing for it - `inst-escalation-lookup`
       1. [x] - `p1` - That parent-minted handler tags the action being routed with this inbound bridge as its arrival edge before forwarding its node to the parent registry's mediator, so the parent's forwarding-entry resolution never re-selects this same edge while re-routing that action; a `next` or `fallback` continuation is routed afresh and is not subject to that action's arrival-edge exclusion - `inst-tag-arrival-edge`
13. [x] - `p1` - **IF** neither a keyed, hierarchy-derived, catch-all, forwarding-entry, nor escalation handler exists for the target - `inst-no-handler`
    1. [x] - `p1` - Fail the node with a missing-handler error inside that node's own failure boundary; the chain selects its declared `fallback`, and where it declares none the chain ends at this node, recorded in the executor's own settlement record — this failure is never re-caught by, and never selects the fallback of, the node that dispatched this continuation - `inst-throw-no-handler`
14. [x] - `p1` - Treat as a chain failure, answered by the chain's declared `fallback` rather than by a refusal, every condition discoverable only while an accepted chain is executing, at the runtime where it occurs: no handler for a well-formed opaque target, an admission failure at the execution target, an invalid action payload found during execution, a route that is inactive or retracted by the time a node is routed through it, a delivery the far side of a hop refuses, a handler failure, a synchronous throw included, and a handler timeout. The class admits no exception: an executed action's outcome is binary, success selects `next` and every other outcome is failure and selects `fallback`, so every node its executing runtime runs to an outcome selects one of the two branches, and where the selected branch is absent the chain ends at that node. Exactly one side answers each node: a refused delivery is the current action's failure at the delivering runtime and is answered by that runtime's `fallback`, while every failure after the far side accepted the node belongs to the far side and is answered by the far side's `fallback` alone. Teardown of the executor holding a chain is not an outcome of any action and therefore not a member of this class: the chain ends with that runtime, because nothing is left to run it - `inst-chain-failure-class`
15. [x] - `p1` - Resolve the per-action timeout from the action's explicit timeout field or, if absent, from the domain's default action timeout - `inst-resolve-timeout`
16. [x] - `p1` - Enforce at the node exactly that per-action timeout — the action's own declared timeout, or the authoritative target domain's default where the action declares none — and rewrite it for no reason whatever: not for the nodes that preceded it, the hops the chain already crossed, the transit those hops already spent, or how long the chain has already been running - `inst-effective-action-bound`
17. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the resolved handler is a cross-hop route — a downward forwarding entry, the upward escalation tier, or the catch-all tier where the child domain it matches lives in another runtime — hand the node over across the hop together with its continuations, the whole remaining sub-chain, and the chain's diagnostic context, in the envelope of the explicitly versioned internal transport. Carry no bound of this runtime's into the hop, since the node is bounded by the timeout resolved at the registry authoritative for its target, and take no reservation for it - `inst-hand-over-node`
    1. [x] - `p1` - Delivery is synchronous and binary: the hand-over call either refuses or accepts, and never both - `inst-delivery-binary`
    2. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the delivery is refused at the call — the bridge the route travels through is inactive or disposed, one released with a revoked link included, no receiver is wired on its far side, the receiving copy does not recognize the internal transport protocol version, or the receiving registry is disposed - `inst-delivery-refused`
       1. [x] - `p1` - Treat the refusal as the current node's failure as observed by this runtime — the hop's unavailability — record its diagnostic here, naming the hop and the refusal's cause, and dispatch the node's `fallback` from this runtime (`inst-dispatch-continuation`); the far side took nothing, so the refusal has no side effect there - `inst-refused-delivery-fallback`
    3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the delivery is accepted - `inst-delivery-accepted`
       1. [x] - `p1` - This runtime is done with the node when the call returns: it holds no reservation or other pending state for it, runs no timer for it, and receives nothing back, leaving the node's branch selection to the receiving registry, which executes the node and dispatches its continuation (`inst-receive-transfer`), so a far side that fails, falls silent, or is torn down strands nothing here - `inst-hand-over-done`
18. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the resolved handler is local — this runtime executes the node against its own target — add the node to the reservation set for that target, unless it already holds the reservation taken for it at acceptance; a runtime never reserves for a node it hands across a hop - `inst-add-inflight`
19. [x] - `p1` - Invoke the resolved handler within the per-action timeout bound - `inst-invoke-within-timeout`
20. [x] - `p1` - Release the node's reservation once its attempt settles under its own bound — in success, failure, or timeout alike — the release being keyed to the attempt settling, not to the handler's own work finishing, which the runtime does not cancel - `inst-remove-inflight`
21. [x] - `p1` - The runtime that executed the action selects `next` where the action succeeded and `fallback` for any other outcome, from that action's own outcome alone — never from the chain's history, its emitter, its origin, the nesting depth, or the hops it crossed — and dispatches the selected continuation from itself as a new dispatch, routed afresh through its own resolution tiers rather than retracing the path the chain took. That new dispatch is not subject to the arrival-edge exclusion that governed re-routing of the action it follows, so a continuation whose target lies back across the edge the chain arrived on travels back across that edge - `inst-dispatch-continuation`
22. [x] - `p1` - **IF** handler execution succeeds - `inst-success`
    1. [x] - `p1` - Append the action type to the execution path - `inst-append-path-success`
    2. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the chain carries a `next` continuation - `inst-has-next`
       1. [x] - `p1` - Recurse into `next` with the updated path - `inst-recurse-success`
    3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** no `next` continuation is declared - `inst-chain-done`
       1. [x] - `p1` - **RETURN** the completed settlement record to the executor's own recursion, never to an emitter - `inst-return-done`
23. [x] - `p1` - **IF** handler execution throws or times out - `inst-failure`
    1. [x] - `p1` - Append the action type to the execution path - `inst-append-path-failure`
    2. [x] - `p1` - **IF** the chain carries a `fallback` continuation - `inst-has-fallback`
       1. [x] - `p1` - Recurse into `fallback` with the updated path - `inst-recurse-fallback-algo`
    3. [x] - `p1` - **IF** no `fallback` continuation is declared - `inst-no-fallback-algo`
       1. [x] - `p1` - End the chain at this node and settle it as non-completed in the executor's own internal record; a continuation's failure is handled entirely within the continuation — it is never re-caught by, and never selects the fallback of, the node that dispatched it - `inst-end-at-node`
24. [x] - `p1` - Record, at the runtime where it occurs, for a refusal and for every node failure alike, a structured diagnostic sufficient to attribute a chain that fails where nobody is watching: the accumulated chain path, the node's target, the refusal or failure class, a correlation identity for the dispatch, and, where the dispatch carried them, the originating lifecycle stage and extension. A refused delivery is recorded by the delivering runtime and a failure after acceptance by the far side; the correlation identity, the origin, and the path so far travel with a sub-chain handed across a hop, so a failure several hops from where the chain was triggered still attributes to the dispatch that started it. The failure class names a refused delivery — the hop unavailable — as a class of its own, recording which cause occurred (an inactive bridge, a disposed bridge, one released with a revoked link included, no receiver wired, an unrecognized internal transport protocol version, or a disposed receiving registry) alongside the hop it names, distinctly from a handler failure, a handler timeout, and a missing handler, because a reader must be able to tell the far side having run the action and failed from the far side having been unable to take it. The class governs only how the failure is named, never how it is routed, so every one of those conditions remains a chain failure the declared `fallback` answers. The destination that receives these diagnostics is substitutable, so what the runtime recorded can be asserted against rather than only read by a person - `inst-diagnostic-record`
25. [ ] - `p1` - A handler that hands a further chain to the acceptance operation starts a new root execution, with its own envelope validation, its own execution-path accumulator, and its own diagnostic context; that execution's effects are not a continuation of the dispatching chain unless the dispatching chain declares them as its own `next` or `fallback` - `inst-new-root-execution`
26. [x] - `p1` - When the registry whose executor holds a chain is disposed, that teardown is a lifetime boundary on every execution the executor holds and never an outcome of any action: a node whose attempt settles after the teardown selects neither `next` nor `fallback`, no continuation is dispatched from that executor for it, and nothing is recorded as a chain failure — no failure class, no fallback — because the chain ends there for lack of anything left to run it. The executor releases the reservations those held executions hold against their targets, so retirement of those targets still drains, and starts no new node after its own teardown. A chain this executor already handed across a hop and that the far side already accepted is unaffected, because this executor held nothing for it at the time of its own teardown - `inst-executor-teardown-ends`

### Registration Propagation, Escalation, and Retraction

- [ ] `p2` - **ID**: `cpt-frontx-algo-mfe-host-communication-registration-propagation`

**Input**: A domain-or-extension admission event carrying a target identifier and its declared action-type id set, at a registry that may or may not hold an inbound bridge; an unmount or mount-failure event for a host extension; a permanent-unregistration event for a host extension, or a disposal event for a registry; a registry-construction event that may occur while a mount is in progress, possibly against an independently loaded copy of this package from the extension's own mount host

**Output**: A newly constructed registry automatically holds the inbound bridge of the extension currently being mounted, if any and if resolvable, without any explicit action by the microfrontend author; every ancestor registry up to and including the shell holds a forwarding entry for the admitted target, or the advertisement was rejected by a collision guard and logged; on the host extension's unmount or mount failure the parent deactivates that extension's bridge, keeping every advertisement propagated through it recorded while refusing each delivery that would travel through it; on the host extension's permanent unregistration or the registry's own disposal, every advertisement propagated through that link is retracted by the parent and the link is revoked, so later deliveries to its targets find no route; in either case a sub-chain a far side accepted before the deactivation or retraction keeps executing there

**Steps**:
1. 2. [ ] - `p1` - **IF** a registry is constructed while the rendezvous point holds a currently-mounting bridge tagged with a protocol version this copy recognizes (that is, the extension's own code builds a further `MfeRegistry` synchronously during its `mount` call) - `inst-adopt-ambient-bridge`
   1. [ ] - `p1` - The newly constructed registry automatically adopts that bridge as its own inbound bridge, with no configuration or method call required from the microfrontend author - `inst-inbound-bridge-auto-adopt`
   2. [ ] - `p1` - The adopting registry publishes, into the same rendezvous entry, a link callback the runtime retains against that host extension — the only channel through which the parent can reach an already-constructed registry to supersede or release its adoption, carrying no importable symbol - `inst-publish-relink-callback`
3. [ ] - `p1` - **IF** no registry is constructed while the rendezvous point holds a currently-mounting bridge (the extension does not build its own nested registry synchronously within `mount`, or is not itself a host), or the rendezvous entry found carries a protocol version this copy does not recognize - `inst-no-ambient-bridge`

[ ] - `p1` - The constructed registry holds no inbound bridge and behaves as a root/shell registry for propagation and escalation purposes; when a mounted extension exists elsewhere on the page and this registry still resolved no bridge, or an unrecognized protocol version was found, log a diagnostic rather than degrading silently - `inst-registry-is-root`

5. [ ] - `p1` - A registry that adopts the link — the first registry to adopt it, or one adopting on a later mount and thereby superseding the previous adopter (step 10.1) — advertises through it every target it currently holds: each domain and extension admitted to it, and every forwarding entry it holds on behalf of its own descendants - `inst-relink-repropagate`
   1. [ ] - `p1` - Where that adoption supersedes an earlier one, the superseded registry discards its record of what the link had accepted from it, so nothing it propagated is counted against the adoption that replaced it, and the adopting registry establishes downward chain delivery through the link and escalates thereafter through the route it carries (step 4) - `inst-relink-downward-delivery`
6. [ ] - `p1` - On admission of a domain or extension, the registry composes a forwarding advertisement consisting of the target identifier and its declared action-type id set, treated as opaque identifiers - `inst-compose-advertisement`

Retraction and deactivation act on routes, not on executions: each stops a route resolving for new deliveries, nothing in flight on a delivering side exists to reject because a delivering runtime holds nothing for a node it handed over, and a sub-chain a far side accepted before the retraction or deactivation keeps executing there under that registry's own rules - `inst-retract-advertisements`

    2. [ ] - `p1` - A link revoked at permanent release refuses all further `propagateAdvertisement`, `retractAdvertisement`, and `escalate` calls, rejecting each explicitly so the caller's own fallback branch runs rather than the call appearing to succeed, so a reference to it retained beyond revocation — by any copy of the runtime — can never resurrect routing to an extension that is no longer registered - `inst-revoked-link-inert`
11. [ ] - `p1` - On a registry's own disposal, it symmetrically retracts every advertisement it had itself propagated through its own inbound bridge, recursively for the whole disposing subtree - `inst-retract-own-advertisements`
12. [ ] - `p1` - A nested registry is linked for as long as its host extension is registered, across any number of mount cycles, because the link outlives every individual mount: a registry the author reuses across a remount keeps the link it already adopted and needs nothing from the parent, and a registry the author rebuilds inside a fresh `mount` call adopts that same still-live link through step 2, superseding its predecessor's adoption — so both patterns are equally supported and neither requires any action from the microfrontend author. The only registry that can never be linked is one whose construction never occurred inside any mount window at all — built at module-evaluation time, or asynchronously after `mount` already returned; it behaves as a root registry (step 3) with a logged diagnostic. The factory-with-cache pattern means a registry built at module-evaluation time — rather than deferred until `mount()` — permanently consumes the one build-slot cache entry for that configuration, precluding any later mount of that extension from linking correctly; a nested host MUST therefore defer its first registry `build()` call until inside its own `mount()` lifecycle method - `inst-nested-registry-lifetime-scope`

## 4. States (CDSL)

### Action State Machine

- [ ] `p2` - **ID**: `cpt-frontx-state-mfe-host-communication-action-lifecycle`

**States**: PENDING, DISPATCHED, SUCCEEDED, FAILED, FALLBACK

**Initial State**: PENDING

**Transitions**:
1. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** PENDING **TO** DISPATCHED **WHEN** a handler is resolved and the action is invoked within its timeout bound - `inst-t-pending-dispatched`
2. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** DISPATCHED **TO** SUCCEEDED **WHEN** handler execution completes without error - `inst-t-dispatched-succeeded`
3. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** DISPATCHED **TO** FAILED **WHEN** handler execution throws an error or the per-action timeout expires - `inst-t-dispatched-failed`
   **Actions**:
   - [ ] - `p1` - Runtime records the action type in the execution path - `inst-failed-record-path`
   - [ ] - `p1` - Runtime releases the node's reservation against its target - `inst-failed-untrack`
   - [ ] - `p1` - **IF** the chain declares a `fallback` continuation - `inst-failed-check-fallback`
     - [ ] - `p1` - Transition the action to FALLBACK and recurse into the fallback chain node - `inst-failed-to-fallback`
   - [ ] - `p1` - **IF** no `fallback` is declared - `inst-failed-no-fallback`
     - [ ] - `p1` - End the chain at this node; the executor settles the chain as non-completed in its own internal record and diagnostics, and nothing reaches an emitter — this failure is never re-caught by, and never selects the fallback of, an ancestor node that already dispatched this continuation - `inst-failed-end-at-node`
4. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** FAILED **TO** FALLBACK **WHEN** the chain declares a fallback continuation that is recursively executed - `inst-t-failed-fallback`
5. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** SUCCEEDED **TO** DISPATCHED **WHEN** the chain declares a `next` continuation and the next action is dispatched - `inst-t-succeeded-dispatched`
6. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** PENDING **TO** FAILED **WHEN** the far side refuses the node's delivery at the call (`inst-delivery-refused`, `inst-refused-delivery-fallback`, `inst-flow-refused-fallback`) - `inst-t-pending-failed-refused`
7. [ ] - `p1` - **FROM** PENDING at the delivering runtime **TO** PENDING at the receiving registry **WHEN** the far side accepts the node's delivery (`inst-delivery-accepted`, `inst-receive-transfer`, `inst-flow-accepted-continues`) - `inst-t-pending-handed-over`
8. [x] - `p1` - **FROM** PENDING or DISPATCHED **TO** no state **WHEN** the registry whose executor holds the action is disposed (`inst-executor-teardown-ends`): the executor's teardown ends that action's lifecycle where it stands, selecting neither SUCCEEDED, FAILED, nor FALLBACK, because teardown is a lifetime boundary and never an outcome of any action - `inst-t-executor-teardown-ends`

## 5. Definitions of Done

### Mediator Keyed Dispatch and Reservation Tracking

- [ ] `p1` - **ID**: `cpt-frontx-dod-mfe-host-communication-mediator-dispatch`

The system **MUST** implement the actions-chains mediator with a keyed `(targetId, actionTypeId)` handler registry and a per-target catch-all tier, executing chains recursively with success and fallback branching, every node bounded solely by its own per-action bound, and reservation tracking that keeps a target's handler registrations in place while reservations against it stand. Handing a chain to the runtime **MUST** be acceptance-only: the operation takes the chain alone, yields nothing an emitter can await for the chain's execution, and reports nothing about what the chain then did, so observation of each action's settlement stays inside the runtime executing that action, satisfying `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-origin-invariant-chain-execution` (MFES-8). A structurally malformed chain — including one whose node graph closes a cycle — an invalid declared per-action timeout, and an emitter dispatch capability unusable at the moment of the call **MUST** each be refused synchronously at that call, never as a returned value or a rejected promise, with no execution accepted; and every condition discoverable only while an accepted chain is executing — no handler for a well-formed opaque target, an admission failure at the execution target, an invalid action payload found during execution, a route that is inactive or retracted by the time a node is routed through it, a delivery the far side of a hop refuses, a handler failure, a synchronous throw included, and a handler timeout — **MUST** instead be a chain failure answered by the `fallback` the chain declared, with no exception: an executed action's outcome **MUST** be binary, success selecting `next` and every other outcome selecting `fallback`. The runtime that executes an action **MUST** select that branch from the action's own outcome alone and **MUST** dispatch the selected continuation from itself as a new dispatch routed afresh through its own resolution tiers, and each node's handler **MUST** be resolved when that node executes, never earlier. An accepted chain **MUST** be owned at each step by the runtime executing its current action and **MUST** live for as long as that runtime's executor holds it: unmounting, destroying, or permanently unregistering the extension that emitted a chain **MUST NOT** end it, since the emitter is only the trigger, and only teardown of the executor holding a chain ends it — a lifetime boundary, not an outcome of any action. Structural validation of the chain envelope **MUST** be the runtime's own, since the injected type-system provider admits individual actions and never the envelope, and action admission **MUST** run at the registry authoritative for the target and inside the failing node's failure boundary, so a target identifier the emitter's own provider does not recognize is never a refusal. Before the accepting call returns, the executor **MUST** have validated the envelope, created its own execution state, and, where the first executable node executes locally, reserved what that node needs, so a chain dispatched alongside a runtime transition reaches its target instead of racing it; the executor **MUST NOT** reserve anything for a first node bound for another runtime. A node holding a reservation against its target **MUST** keep that target's handler reachable until the node's attempt settles, even where the target is retired in the meantime: retirement **MUST** exclude the target from new dispatches only and **MUST NOT** turn a standing reservation into a missing-handler failure, and the retired target's handler registrations **MUST** be removed once the reservations standing against it drain, without the retiring transition waiting for them. The bound enforced at a node **MUST** be that node's own per-action timeout — the action's own declared timeout, or the authoritative target domain's default where the action declares none — and **MUST NOT** be rewritten in light of the nodes that preceded it, the hops the chain already crossed, the transit those hops spent, or how long the chain has already been running; a declared per-action timeout **MUST** be a positive, finite, integer count of milliseconds a platform timer schedules as written, refused rather than clamped where it is not. That every action of an accepted chain reaches a settlement at the runtime executing it **MUST** rest on refusing a cyclic chain before acceptance, so that an accepted chain is a finite acyclic graph and every path through it is a finite sequence of individually bounded attempts; no bound on a chain's total running time is enforced or implied. Where a node's target lives in another runtime, the current runtime **MUST** hand the node over across the hop together with its continuations and the chain's diagnostic context and **MUST** then be done with it: it holds no reservation or other pending state for that node, applies no bound to it, and receives nothing back. Delivery across a hop **MUST** be synchronous and binary — refused at the call or accepted, never both. A refused delivery — an inactive or disposed bridge, no receiver wired, an internal transport protocol version the receiving copy does not recognize, or a disposed receiving registry — **MUST** be the current node's failure at the delivering runtime, which dispatches the node's `fallback` from itself, and **MUST** leave no side effect at the far side; a copy meeting an unrecognized transport protocol version **MUST** record a diagnostic naming the version it met and the version it implements. Acceptance **MUST** validate what was handed over and take and reserve what the node needs before the delivery call returns, **MUST NOT** run handler code on the delivering runtime's call stack, and **MUST** transfer the sub-chain, so every later failure — a missing handler, an admission failure, a handler that throws, synchronously included, or a timeout — is the far side's, answered by its own `fallback`, and nothing after acceptance surfaces back through the delivery call. Because no emitter observes an outcome, the runtime where a refusal or node failure occurs **MUST** record for it a structured diagnostic carrying the chain path, the node's target, the refusal or failure class, a correlation identity, and, where the dispatch carried them, the originating lifecycle stage and extension, to a destination that is substitutable so what was recorded can be asserted against; the correlation identity, the origin, and the path so far **MUST** travel with a sub-chain handed across a hop. That failure class **MUST** name a refused delivery — the hop unavailable, together with which of its causes occurred — as a class of its own, distinct from a handler failure, a handler timeout, and a missing handler, while every one of those conditions **MUST** still be answered by the chain's declared `fallback`. A handler that hands a further chain to the acceptance operation **MUST** start a new root execution whose effects are not a continuation of the dispatching chain unless that chain declared them as its own `next` or `fallback`. Action admission is delegated to the injected type-system provider; the mediator carries no type-format knowledge. The property channel passed through the bridge surface carries no solution-specific identifiers, satisfying `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-no-solution-shared-properties` (MFES-2). The reachability guarantee behind this handler resolution MUST hold transitively across any nesting depth, not just at a single hop, and MUST hold when a nested registry is built against its own independently loaded copy of this package rather than sharing an evaluated module with its host: a registry constructed while an extension's `mount` call is synchronously in progress MUST automatically adopt that extension's bridge as its own inbound bridge, coordinated through a realm-global, version-namespaced rendezvous rather than shared module state, with no configuration or method call from the microfrontend author; a registry that resolves no bridge, or resolves a rendezvous entry tagged with an unrecognized protocol version, MUST behave as a root registry and MUST log a diagnostic rather than degrading silently; admitting a domain or extension MUST automatically propagate a forwarding advertisement through the registry's inbound bridge to every ancestor up to and including the shell, with a collision guard that MUST accept, without rejection and without a diagnostic, an advertisement re-stating an entry the ancestor already holds for the very edge that advertisement arrived on, and MUST reject and log one whose target identifier collides with an entry the ancestor holds locally or for a different edge; resolution MUST add a downward forwarding-entry tier and, when the registry holds an inbound bridge, a final upward-escalation tier reached through that bridge and carried by the link the parent registry minted once for that host extension rather than identified by the child testing the bridge's concrete class, both resolving — as the catch-all tier does where the child domain it matches lives in another runtime — to the cross-hop route shape rather than to a plain `ActionHandler`, and both reusing the chain-execution machinery so per-node bounds and branching apply at every runtime that executes a node, while reservation tracking applies at the runtime that executes each node and never at one that only hands it across a hop; a forwarded or escalated node MUST be bounded by the timeout resolved at the registry authoritative for its target, unchanged by the hops the chain crossed to reach it; an action forwarded or escalated across a bridge MUST be tagged with that arrival edge so forwarding-entry resolution never re-routes that same action onto that edge, while a continuation dispatched afresh from the runtime that executed the action MUST NOT be excluded from it; and on a host extension's permanent unregistration or a registry's own disposal, the parent registry MUST retract every advertisement propagated through that link, so later deliveries to its targets find no route. A host extension's unmount or mount failure MUST NOT retract those advertisements: the parent MUST deactivate that extension's bridge instead, keeping every entry recorded through it while refusing each delivery that would travel through it, so the delivering runtime's `fallback` runs rather than the dispatch silently succeeding. Retraction and deactivation MUST act on routes only: each stops new deliveries through the route, and neither touches a sub-chain a far side already accepted, which keeps executing there. The link the parent mints for a host extension MUST be minted once, at that extension's first mount, and stay live across every subsequent mount, so a registry the author reuses across a remount keeps routing without any further act by the parent; a registry that adopts that link MUST propagate every target it currently holds, both its own admissions and the forwarding entries it holds on behalf of its own descendants, and MUST escalate thereafter through the escalation route and arrival-edge tagging that link carries, while a registry whose adoption a later mount supersedes MUST be unlinked and MUST clear its own record of what it had propagated; and a link revoked at permanent unregistration or disposal MUST be inert, refusing all further propagation, retraction, and escalation through it explicitly rather than silently, so no ancestor can ever acquire or retain a forwarding entry pointing at an extension that is no longer registered. This composition MUST introduce zero growth to the package's public surface, satisfying `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-cross-nesting-reachability` (MFES-6): no new capability method is added to `MfeRegistry` or any other exported type to support propagation, the collision guard, escalation, the hand-over across a hop, loop containment, deactivation, or retraction, and the rendezvous protocol carries no importable symbol.

## 6. Acceptance Criteria

- [x] The actions-chains mediator resolves a handler by the `(targetId, actionTypeId)` pair and falls back to the per-target catch-all handler when no specific pair matches
- [x] When neither a keyed, hierarchy-derived, nor catch-all handler matches, resolution continues through a downward forwarding entry and, when the registry has an inbound bridge, a final upward-escalation handler, before treating the target as unresolved
- [ ] The runtime executing each node follows the `next` continuation on success and the `fallback` continuation on any other outcome, selected from that node's own outcome alone, and dispatches that continuation from itself, with every node it executes bounded solely by that node's own per-action bound
- [ ] Handing a chain to the runtime through the registry facade or the child bridge accepts it or refuses it and yields nothing an emitter can await for the chain's execution; neither operation takes a per-call execution-options or timeout argument
- [ ] A structurally malformed chain, a chain whose node graph closes a cycle, a declared per-action timeout that is not a positive, finite, integer count of milliseconds a platform timer schedules as written, and a disposed, already-inactive or unwired emitter dispatch capability are each refused synchronously at the call — the invalid timeout refused rather than clamped — with no execution accepted and no rejected promise left behind
- [ ] An unresolved well-formed target, an admission failure at the execution target, an invalid action payload found during execution, a route that is inactive or retracted by the time a node is routed through it, a delivery the far side of a hop refuses, a handler failure, a synchronous throw included, and a handler timeout each select the chain's declared `fallback` instead of refusing the call, with no exception: every node its executing runtime runs to an outcome selects `next` or `fallback`
- [ ] An accepted chain runs under whichever runtime is executing its current action for as long as that runtime's executor holds it: unmounting, destroying, or permanently unregistering the extension that emitted a chain ends no chain that extension emitted, a runtime that handed the chain across a hop holds nothing for it, and only teardown of the executor holding a chain ends it
- [ ] Action admission runs at the registry authoritative for the addressed target and inside the failing node's failure boundary, so a target identifier the emitter's own type-system provider does not recognize executes and fails as a chain node rather than being refused at the call
- [ ] An accepted chain is acyclic, so every path an execution follows through it is a finite sequence of individually bounded attempts and every action reaches a settlement at the runtime executing it; no ceiling on a chain's total running time is enforced
- [ ] The bound enforced at a node is that node's own per-action timeout — its declared timeout, or the authoritative target domain's default where it declares none — and is never rewritten in light of preceding nodes, hops already crossed, transit already spent, or how long the chain has been running
- [ ] The accepting call has validated the chain envelope, created the executor's state, and, where the first executable node executes locally, reserved what that node needs before it returns, so a chain dispatched alongside a teardown transition reaches its target rather than racing it; nothing is reserved for a first node bound for another runtime
- [ ] Every refusal and every node failure is recorded, by the runtime where it occurs, as a structured diagnostic carrying the chain path, the node's target, the refusal or failure class, a correlation identity and, where the dispatch carried them, the originating lifecycle stage and extension, to a substitutable destination a test can assert against; the correlation identity, origin, and path so far travel with a sub-chain handed across a hop, so a failure several hops from the trigger site still attributes to the dispatch that started it
- [ ] A refused delivery is recorded under a class of its own — the hop unavailable, naming the hop and which cause occurred: an inactive bridge, a disposed bridge, no receiver wired, an unrecognized internal transport protocol version, or a disposed receiving registry — distinct from a handler failure, a handler timeout, and a missing handler, and each such cause still selects the chain's declared `fallback`
- [ ] A handler that hands a further chain to the acceptance operation starts a new root execution whose effects are not a continuation of the dispatching chain unless that chain declared them as its own `next` or `fallback`
- [ ] Reservation tracking keeps a target's handler registrations in place while reservations against it stand; a runtime reserves only for a node it executes against its own target and never for a node it hands across a hop
- [ ] A node reserved against its target — at acceptance or before its attempt — reaches that target's handler and settles even where the target is retired between the reservation and the node's execution; retirement excludes the target from new dispatches only, and its handler registrations are removed once the reservations standing against it drain, without the retiring transition waiting for them
- [x] Action admission is delegated to the injected type-system provider; no type-format literals appear in the mediator
- [x] The child bridge surface is exactly the four capability methods `executeActionsChain`, `subscribeToProperty`, `getProperty`, and `registerActionHandler` plus exactly two readonly identity properties, `extDomainId` (the GTS identifier of the domain the extension is mounted into) and `extensionId` (the extension's own GTS identifier); the parent bridge surface is exactly `instanceId` and `dispose()`, unchanged regardless of nesting depth
- [x] Both child-bridge identity properties hold the extension's and its domain's own GTS identifiers and are stable for the extension's whole registration lifetime, and the bridge's active/inactive state appears on no public surface of the abstract `ChildMfeBridge`/`ParentMfeBridge` contracts; the concrete child-bridge implementation that holds that state is not exported from the package barrel, so the members through which it inspects that state reach no consumer
- [x] The property channel carries no solution-specific shared-property identifiers, satisfying `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-no-solution-shared-properties` (MFES-2)
- [x] Child domain forwarding uses the catch-all handler tier in the parent mediator, forwarding actions through the bridge transport without the parent enumerating the child's action vocabulary
- [ ] A registry constructed while an extension's `mount` call is synchronously in progress automatically adopts that extension's bridge as its own inbound bridge via a realm-global rendezvous, requiring no configuration or method call by the microfrontend author, and this holds even when the registry and its host extension are evaluating independently loaded copies of this package; a registry constructed outside any such window holds no inbound bridge and behaves as a root registry with no diagnostic logged (the normal path for every top-level/shell registry construction), while a registry resolving a rendezvous entry carrying an unrecognized protocol version, or a bridge with no link attached, likewise holds no inbound bridge but logs a diagnostic
- [x] Admitting a domain or extension automatically propagates a forwarding advertisement through each registry's inbound bridge, so that every ancestor up to and including the shell ends up holding a forwarding entry for the admitted target, without any explicit registration action by the microfrontend author
- [ ] An ancestor that already holds a local registration, or a forwarding entry recorded for a different edge, for an advertised target identifier rejects the colliding advertisement, logs a diagnostic, and does not propagate it further; an advertisement re-stating an entry that ancestor already holds for the very edge it arrived on is accepted as an idempotent no-op, neither rejected nor logged
- [x] A chain unresolved by the keyed, hierarchy-derived, local catch-all, and forwarding-entry tiers escalates upward through the registry's inbound bridge to the parent's mediator, except at the shell, which has no further ancestor to escalate to, using an escalation handler the parent registry minted once for that host extension rather than one the child identifies by testing the bridge's concrete class
- [ ] A node whose target lives in another runtime — reached through a downward forwarding entry, the upward escalation tier, or child-domain forwarding — is handed over together with its continuations and the chain's diagnostic context, after which the delivering runtime holds no reservation or pending state for it, runs no timer for it, and receives nothing back; the node is bounded by the timeout resolved at the registry authoritative for its target, and a node whose handler is slow but inside that bound succeeds across one and several hops exactly as it succeeds locally
- [ ] Delivery across a hop either refuses at the call or accepts, never both: a refused delivery — an inactive or disposed bridge, no receiver wired, an unrecognized internal transport protocol version, or a disposed receiving registry — fails the node at the delivering runtime, which dispatches its `fallback` from itself, and leaves no side effect at the far side; a copy meeting an unrecognized transport protocol version records a diagnostic naming the version it met and the version it implements
- [ ] Acceptance at the receiving registry validates what was handed over and reserves what the node needs before the delivery call returns, and runs no handler code on the delivering runtime's call stack; after acceptance every failure — a missing handler, an admission failure, a handler that throws synchronously or later, or a timeout — is answered by the far side's `fallback` and never also by the delivering runtime's
- [ ] An action is never re-routed onto the bridge edge it most recently arrived on, preventing an escalate-then-forward loop between the same two registries, while a continuation dispatched afresh from the runtime that executed the action is routed through that runtime's own tiers and, where its target lies back across that edge, travels back across it and reaches it
- [ ] A host extension's permanent unregistration, or a registry's own disposal, causes the parent registry to revoke that link and retract every forwarding advertisement propagated through it, regardless of whether the registry's own author disposes it, so later deliveries to its targets find no route; retraction rejects nothing in flight at a delivering runtime, which holds nothing for a node it handed over, and a sub-chain a far side accepted before the retraction keeps executing there
- [ ] A host extension's unmount or mount failure leaves every forwarding advertisement propagated through its link in place and deactivates its bridge instead; a delivery through that inactive bridge is refused at the call with a target-inactive cause, so the delivering runtime dispatches the node's `fallback`, while a sub-chain the far side accepted before the deactivation is left executing there; the next mount reactivates the same bridge with its handler registrations and property subscriptions still live unless the microfrontend's own `unmount()` hook withdrew them
- [x] A registry an author reuses across a remount keeps the link it already adopted and continues to route with no further act by the parent; a registry the author rebuilds inside a fresh `mount` call adopts that same still-live link, supersedes its predecessor's adoption — which is unlinked and clears its own propagation record — and advertises every target it holds; both patterns route correctly, and no dispatch is ever delivered through a bridge whose extension is no longer registered
- [x] A link revoked at a host extension's permanent unregistration or a registry's disposal is inert: a registry that retained a reference to it can neither propagate nor escalate through it, each such call being rejected explicitly rather than silently ignored, so no ancestor can acquire a forwarding entry pointing at an extension that is no longer registered; `retractAdvertisement` is the one exception, an idempotent silent no-op on a revoked link, because the revoking parent has already performed that retraction itself
- [ ] Propagation, the collision guard, escalation, the hand-over across a hop, loop containment, deactivation, and retraction introduce no new capability method or exported type anywhere in the package's public surface, satisfying `cpt-frontx-constraint-mfes-cross-nesting-reachability` (MFES-6); the inbound-bridge rendezvous is verified, by a test exercising two independently loaded copies of this package rather than one shared module graph, to adopt correctly and never misattribute one extension's bridge to another's registry
